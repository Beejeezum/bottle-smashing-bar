# The Bottle Smashing Bar — design notes

## The premise

It is the last night of the last honest bar in town. The landlord, Mr. Pemberton, wants to turn the Smash Range into condos and will change the locks at 2 a.m. unless the jar on the bar is full. Every smash puts money in the jar. The regulars dare you for more.

Everyone in the bar is a living neon sign. So are you: the hand holding your glass is neon tube.

## The night, in three beats

1. **9 PM, walk in.** Sal the bartender explains the jar, pours you a free lager, and sends you to break something.
2. **The dares.** Each regular has one. Finishing a dare and telling them about it pays into the jar and gives you a permanent perk. The clock on screen is the jar: it reads 2 AM when the jar is full.
3. **Last call.** The bell rings, Pemberton walks in, and his personal collection is wheeled onto red pedestals in the range. Smash all five and he gives up the building. After that it is "after hours": free play, everything pays double.

## How drinking works

- Sal builds each drink on the bar: the glass fills, slides over, and waits there until you are close enough to take it.
- Drinks have garnish: ice in the whiskey and water, foam on the stout, an olive in the martini, lime on the tequila.
- Drinking tips your head back and dims the room behind the glass. Strong drinks burn (warm flash, cough). Alcohol lands a few seconds after you swallow, not instantly.
- Drunkenness stacks up in layers: camera sway, double vision, a walk that drifts, mouse-look that arrives late so the view swims, random lurches, hiccups, and slow heavy blinks. The jukebox slows down and detunes with you.
- Water sobers you up. The score multiplier rises with how drunk you are, so sobering up is a real trade.

## Ideas not built yet

- **The bathroom.** A bladder meter, a graffiti-covered restroom, and a mirror where you finally see your own neon self.
- **More nights.** Night two: Pemberton sends a health inspector you have to keep distracted. Night three: a rival bar's bowling team shows up for a throw-off.
- **Throwing styles.** Overhand, underhand lob and sidearm skip, each better for different targets.
- **Jukebox control.** Pay the jar to pick the song; each one changes the range (slow song, slow ducks).
- **Regulars who move.** Patrons wander to the range to watch, cheer and heckle from the counter.
- **A proper close.** A morning-after screen: your tab, your best streak, and what the regulars remember about you.
- **Saving progress** between visits, and touch controls for phones.
